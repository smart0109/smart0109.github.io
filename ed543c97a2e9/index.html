The Integration Pitch

One Record Across 40 Locations

Dr. Hergan sits at the intersection of two systems that rarely talk to each other cleanly: as Chief of Orthopedic Surgery at Middlesex Hospital he runs a hospital-grade surgical service line, and as one of Spire Orthopedic Partners' first Chief Medical Officers he now carries that same clinical-operations lens across 170+ physicians and 40 sites that each joined Spire with their own EHR, scheduling, and billing stack intact — by design, to protect the clinical autonomy Spire was built on.

That design choice is also the integration problem. Spire's new CEO, Dr. Shaden Marzouk, has framed this as an active growth phase, not a steady state — which means more practices, more brands, and more disconnected systems arriving on a platform that already runs MRI suites and five ambulatory surgery centers across four states. Every new partnership widens the gap between 'physicians keep their systems' and 'the platform needs one view of the patient.'

Vorro sits in that gap. Instead of forcing every affiliated practice onto a single EHR — the fastest way to reopen the autonomy debate Hergan and Vadasdi were appointed to manage — Vorro connects each practice's existing record, ASC schedule, imaging system, and payer workflow into one interoperable layer, with ambient documentation and coding support layered on top to give back the time Vadasdi says administrative growth is eating into. For a CMO managing unification across brands rather than consolidation onto one system, that's the difference between a multi-year EHR replacement and a connectivity project that starts now.
