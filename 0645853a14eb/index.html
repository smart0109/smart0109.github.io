The Integration Pitch

Stop building pipes. Start building product.

Medici's model depends on connecting patients to their own doctors and linking those doctors into whatever systems the patient's care already runs on — Hello Doctor's patient base, EZMed/SpesNet's practice and billing workflows, the physician network inherited from the DocbookMD acquisition. Today, each of those is a separately engineered connection: custom parsing, one-off mapping logic, and an integration-specific bug queue that Duncan's team owns and maintains indefinitely.

Vorro replaces that pipe-by-pipe model with a single FHIR/HL7/C-CDA integration layer sitting behind Medici's existing APIs. New partner clinics, practice-management systems, or medical schemes connect through one standardized surface instead of a bespoke build each time — cutting the engineering lead time on every new SA or US integration and shrinking the number of brittle, hand-maintained interfaces the team is responsible for keeping alive.

That frees Duncan's roadmap for what actually differentiates Medici — ambient scribing, live translation, and patient summarization layered on top of consults — instead of unpaid engineering debt spent re-solving the same interoperability problem for every new partner. Fewer point-to-point integrations also means a smaller, easier-to-audit API surface, which matters directly for a platform built on HIPAA compliance and operating under POPIA in South Africa.
