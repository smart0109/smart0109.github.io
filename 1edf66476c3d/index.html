Angles for the conversation

Three openings that land with a cancer program, in the order they usually hurt.

1

Therapy stalls on prior authorization

Prior auth and financial assistance bounce between the EHR, payer portals and the specialty pharmacy, so patients wait days to start. Vorro wires the EHR to the payer services and the dispensing pharmacy so status, benefits and fills sync on their own.

2

Radiation oncology is a silo

ARIA and MOSAIQ live apart from the medical oncology EHR and billing, which forces duplicate charting and quietly drops charges. A bidirectional interface keeps the record, the schedule and charge capture in step.

3

Pathology still arrives as a fax

Outside labs and pathology come by fax or portal and get scanned in, delaying treatment decisions and tumor board prep. Vorro lands those results structured in the EHR automatically.

Why Vorro, why now

  • Any to any integration, so the EHR, the rad onc system and the labs stay exactly where they are

Acronyms, in plain English

The alphabet soup this brief uses, decoded.

HL7 The long standing messaging standard most clinical systems already speak.
FHIR The modern API standard for health data. Newer, cleaner, and what payers and regulators increasingly require.
PA Prior authorization. The payer approval a drug or procedure needs before it can proceed.
ARIA and MOSAIQ The two dominant radiation oncology information systems, from Varian and Elekta.
PACS Picture archiving and communication system. Where imaging studies live.
OCM and EOM Medicare oncology payment models. Both demand reporting pulled from several systems.
Tumor registry The reportable cancer case record every program is required to maintain.
EiPaaS Enterprise integration platform as a service. The category Vorro sits in.
