The Integration Pitch

Why Vorro, Why Now

Dr. Ziad Dimachkie leads clinical operations at WVU Medicine Uniontown Hospital, a 24/7 acute-care flagship in Fayette County that runs on the same Epic instance as the rest of West Virginia's largest health system — 25 hospitals and more than 35,000 employees strong. That scale is a strength for standardized care, but it also means every referral, every legacy chart from a newly affiliated facility, and every specialist note has to reconcile cleanly inside Epic for clinicians — and for Peak Health, WVU Medicine's own payer — to actually use it.

The highest-leverage moment is now: in November 2025, WVU Medicine committed $800 million to acquire and modernize Independence Health System's five hospitals across southwestern Pennsylvania — Uniontown Hospital's own backyard. Community hospitals joining a large Epic system rarely arrive with clean data; they arrive with faxes, PDFs, mismatched identifiers, and years of records living outside Care Everywhere. Every month that gap persists is a month of manual reconciliation for Dr. Dimachkie's clinical staff and a month of delayed claims for revenue cycle.

Vorro turns that reconciliation problem into a connected pipeline — HL7, FHIR, C-CDA, and X12 flowing cleanly between Epic, referring and newly affiliated facilities, diagnostics, and payers like Peak Health, with ambient documentation and real-time translation layered on top so clinicians spend less time on the chart and more time with patients. For a CMO managing a hospital at the center of West Virginia's largest health system expansion, that's the difference between integration as a multi-year IT project and integration as something that just works from day one.
