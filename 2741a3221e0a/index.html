The Integration Pitch

Stop Building HL7 Interfaces One Hospital at a Time

Barak's team already owns the hard part: an FDA-cleared, CE-marked point-of-care platform that MeMed's own materials say "connects seamlessly with LIS systems." But seamless on paper still means a bespoke interface project per site in practice — a different LIS vendor, a different HL7v2 profile, a different EHR's ADT feed, repeated at every new hospital, ED, or lab that adopts MeMed Key. That's engineering hours spent on connectivity plumbing instead of the next assay, the next FDA submission, or MeMed Severity's rollout.

As deployment scales through Beckman Coulter's distribution network and MeMed's biobank-fed AI roadmap pulls in more real-time clinical data, the number of point-to-point interfaces MeMed's engineering org has to build and maintain grows linearly with every new site — a classic integration tax that compounds instead of amortizing.

Vorro replaces that one-off interface work with a single integration layer: connect once via FHIR, HL7, or REST, and every hospital LIS or EHR on the other end — Epic, Oracle Health, MEDITECH, or a regional system — speaks back through the same engine. For a CTO also carrying product, quality, regulatory, and clinical affairs, that's fewer custom builds per go-live, faster site activation timelines, and an engineering team freed up to ship diagnostics rather than maintain plumbing.
