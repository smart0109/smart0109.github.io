The Integration Pitch

Why Vorro, Why Now

MiBA's business model depends on connecting to more EHRs, more labs, and more partner systems every quarter — each new community oncology practice, biomarker lab, or pharma partnership is another data pipeline that has to be built, normalized, and kept running. That's the unglamorous infrastructure work sitting underneath every press release about a new alliance.

As MiBA scales past its core AON relationship into independent practices, payers, and additional genomic-testing partners, the number of point-to-point integrations MiBA's team has to build and maintain grows linearly with every deal it signs — and each one is a distraction from the analytics and RWE product MiBA actually sells.

Vorro gives MiBA a single interoperability layer — FHIR, HL7, C-CDA, and REST APIs — to onboard new EHRs, labs, and partner systems in a fraction of the time, so engineering effort goes toward MiBA's data products instead of custom point-to-point plumbing for every new partnership. For a Managing Partner steering MiBA's growth strategy, that's the difference between integration work capping deal velocity and integration work scaling right alongside it.
