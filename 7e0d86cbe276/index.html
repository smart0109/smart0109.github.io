The Integration Pitch

Why this matters at the board level

Frontier Direct Care has grown roughly tenfold in two years and just added three new clinic markets on top of an $20M Series B — the kind of trajectory that outruns whatever data plumbing was built for a smaller footprint. As a board member, Dr. Bricker isn't the one troubleshooting integrations day to day, but he is one of the people accountable for whether the company's technology foundation can actually support the growth the board approved capital for.

That's the oversight question interoperability answers: not "does the EHR work," but "will member data, employer eligibility feeds, referral outcomes, and pharmacy records stay connected and auditable as Frontier adds its next ten clinics and its next fifty employer accounts." Vorro exists to be the connective layer between a proprietary care platform like Frontier's in-house EHR and the employer systems, referral partners, virtual care tools, and pharmacy networks around it — so growth doesn't quietly outpace the infrastructure underneath it.

Given Dr. Bricker's own track record building and scaling Compass Professional Health Services and his ongoing public work explaining exactly where healthcare cost and data friction come from, he's well positioned to sponsor a conversation between Frontier's technology leadership and Vorro about whether the current integration approach — built fast, in-house, to hit a launch date — is the one that should carry the company through its next stage of scale, or whether a dedicated interoperability layer reduces risk as the clinic count and employer count keep climbing.
