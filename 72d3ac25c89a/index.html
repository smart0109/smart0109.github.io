The Temple Health family

Where Fox Chase sits

Fox Chase is one of six hospitals within Temple Health. All six work from a single shared patient record, held at the parent level. That structure is the starting point for any conversation about data. It means information created at one site is, in principle, already reachable from another.

02

Inside Fox Chase

The teams, and what each one holds

Fox Chase has been an NCI-designated comprehensive cancer center since 1974, with roots reaching back to 1904. Today it treats 18,500 new patients a year across six locations while running more than 80 research laboratories, and it carries three missions at once: caring for patients, teaching through the Lewis Katz School of Medicine, and producing research that changes practice.

Each of those missions runs on the same underlying information. And that information is held by the teams who create it, each in its own system.

The picture today

Excellent systems, built separately

Because these systems were never designed to talk to one another, the connections between them are made by people: reading one screen, exporting a file, sending an email, or entering the same fact a second time.

Recorded twice

A result recorded for care is recorded again for research. Same fact, same day, two people.

Stops at one desk

Information arrives with whoever needed it first. Anyone else must be told, sent it, or fetch it.

Assembled by hand

Reporting that spans systems is rebuilt manually each time it falls due.

Multiplied by site

Six locations means each pattern above happens six times, in six slightly different ways.

06

Clinical trials, linked end to end

Every team that touches a trial, on one path

Clinical trials are a coordination challenge. At Fox Chase, trials move across the Office of Clinical Research, the Study Activation Unit, the IRB, the trials office and patient navigators, while relying on Temple's research administration for grants, budgets and contracts. Each function runs its own systems, primarily OnCore, eIRB, REDCap and the Study Statusboard.

Fox Chase has already shown the value of coordination: its Study Activation Unit reduced the median time to open a trial by 182 days between 2021 and 2023, bringing it to under 90 days by aligning submissions, operations, budgets and contracts.

Vorro applies the same principle to the data: connecting trial information across Fox Chase and Temple so it reaches patients, hospitals and departments without manual handoffs.

07

The academic mission runs on data, too

Fox Chase, the Katz School of Medicine, and Temple University

Fox Chase is more than a hospital: it is a research and teaching institution integrated with the Lewis Katz School of Medicine and Temple University. Faculty, trainees, grants and publications span institutions, creating constant flows of information that are often coordinated manually.

Take SPEECH, jointly run by Fox Chase, the Katz School of Medicine and Hunter College, with NCI funding. Three institutions, two states, one grant, and investigators, trainees, projects and publications must be coordinated and reported across them each cycle.

That is a data problem at the heart of the academic mission, and exactly where a governed data layer can remove manual coordination.

This is live for us today. Vorro already runs this same academic-and-clinical integration for a university hospital in production. It is the same shape of problem Fox Chase is describing here, and it is one we have already solved and are running now.

What the layer does

Six jobs, done once

Connect

Pre-built connectors and standard interfaces (HL7 and FHIR, APIs, X12, DICOM and files) so each system joins once, not per project.

Normalize

Information arriving in each sender's own shape is brought to one consistent structure your teams can rely on.

Map

Fields are matched to the form or record that needs them, the same way every time.

Route

A result arriving once reaches everyone entitled to it: the study record, the registry, analytics, and every site.

Monitor

Every movement is visible and every failure is caught, rather than discovered when a report falls due.

Govern

Defined, permissioned and logged end to end, HIPAA and SOC 2 certified, so what your teams do with the data is reviewable and reproducible.

Nothing is replaced. Epic remains your record. Your trial system remains your trial system. Tools your teams have built stay in place and are fed better inputs. Vorro sits underneath, and its only job is movement.

09

Why the time matters

Funded work carries a deadline

When a program is funded by a grant, the evidence it produces is not optional, and it is not once.

Fox Chase's RECONNECT program, five years of funded work to widen access to clinical trials for underrepresented patients, has to demonstrate its effect. That means enrollment and screening figures, broken down and assembled across every site, produced on schedule, for as long as the program runs.

That duty exists regardless of how the data is gathered. The only question is whether producing it costs a week of skilled time each cycle, or whether the answer is simply available when it is asked for.
