Why now: ACS just committed to a multiyear overhaul of how registry data moves from hospital EHRs into its national quality programs — starting with Epic, but explicitly planned to expand to "additional electronic medical records providers and technology partners over time." That's the hard part: every non-Epic hospital in the 700+-site NSQIP network still needs a reliable, standards-based way to get structured clinical data out of its own EHR and into ACS's registries without a bespoke interface engineering effort for each one.

The Integration Pitch

Built for the engineering reality behind the registry migration

The Epic Community Registries rollout solves the easy 40% of this problem — Epic-on-Epic data capture. The other 60% of NSQIP's 700+ hospitals, plus every future registry ACS wants to automate, run on Cerner, MEDITECH, Allscripts, and a long tail of smaller or legacy systems that Epic's registry pipe doesn't touch. That's a point-to-point interface project multiplied by every non-Epic site, indefinitely, unless the ingestion layer itself is standards-based.

That's the plumbing problem Vorro is built to remove, not add to. A single FHIR/HL7/C-CDA integration layer sits between ACS's registries and any hospital's EHR — Epic or not — normalizing whatever a site sends into the structured format NSQIP, the Cancer Database, and MBSAQIP already expect, so your team isn't hand-building and maintaining a custom interface for every EMR vendor in the network.

For a CIO's org, that's engineering hours redirected from interface maintenance to the registry expansion itself — plus a documentation layer (ambient scribe, coding, patient summary) that can plug into whatever chart data does make it through, without ACS having to build or staff that separately.
