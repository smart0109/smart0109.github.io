The Integration Pitch

Documentation integrity for a plant under the microscope

Indoco's Goa campus runs three regulators at once — USFDA, UK-MHRA, and EU GMP (most recently Malta's Medicines Authority) — and the last two years show exactly how much rides on paperwork: a warning letter and OAI classification at sister Goa units in late 2024, then a clean EU GMP certificate for Plant I in mid-2026. Inspectors don't stop at batch records. They sample deviation logs, training records, and the health, safety, and statutory documentation (ESI filings, medical-exam records, factory medical-center logs) that a Factory Medical Officer's function generates every day — and any of it can turn into an observation if it's incomplete, inconsistent, or hard to produce on demand.

Vorro doesn't touch your GMP or quality systems — it sits at the documentation layer around them. Ambient capture turns factory medical-center visits, worker health-surveillance exams, and incident notes into clean, structured, timestamped records instead of handwritten registers or scattered spreadsheets, with coded output and real-time translation for a multilingual shop floor. The same integration layer that connects a hospital's EHR (FHIR, HL7, REST APIs, webhooks) connects just as easily to a plant's occupational-health register, to SAP SuccessFactors HRMS, or to a QMS deviation log — closing the exact documentation gaps that show up in warning letters, quietly, well before the next inspection.

For Dr. Arlekar's function, that means fewer hours lost to manual transcription and register-keeping, a defensible audit trail for every occupational-health encounter, and one less variable in the site's next USFDA or EU GMP visit — at a campus where the cost of a documentation gap has already been demonstrated.
