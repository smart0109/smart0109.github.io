One integration layer for kidney screening across 10+ countries, not one per hospital

Today's call centered on connecting HelloKidney's diagnostic operations to local hospital systems as the platform scales region by region. That's the problem Vorro is built to solve underneath the rollout.

Prepared following the Jul 7, 2026 intro call with HelloKidney

One connection, many hospitals

HelloKidney's UACR and creatinine results need to land in each local hospital's own EHR/HIS format — HL7, FHIR, CCD, or a legacy feed. One Vorro connection replaces a bespoke build per hospital, per country.

Clinical credibility, operational gap

The Kidney International Reports validation and KDIGO-2024 alignment solve trust. The harder problem is getting results delivered automatically into the ordering hospital's own record so they trigger the right care pathway.

Built for a lean team

A 20-40 person team can't maintain bespoke per-hospital integration engineering while scaling into new countries. A no-code, database-agnostic model keeps integration cost from scaling linearly with hospital count.

Who they are

HelloKidney is an AI-powered point-of-care platform for early chronic kidney disease detection — a smartphone-based UACR urine test, a handheld fingerstick creatinine analyzer, and KDIGO-2024-aligned clinical decision support, used by 500+ healthcare professionals across 10+ countries.

Where Vorro fits

HelloKidney solves screening and clinical decision support. But every hospital, clinic, or government screening program is its own integration project — a different EHR, a different format, a different country's compliance regime. Vorro is the integration layer that turns that into a config change instead of a rebuild each time HelloKidney enters a new region.

Angles for the conversation

1

One build, not one per hospital

HelloKidney's 10+-country footprint means the integration problem multiplies with every new hospital relationship. A no-code, database-agnostic connection replaces a bespoke HL7/FHIR build per site — the same pattern already running across 250+ health systems and 1,000+ facilities.

2

From validated science to validated delivery

The clinical validation is done — published, KDIGO-aligned, WHO-listed. The next credibility gap is operational: proving results reach the ordering clinician's own system reliably. Audit-trailed, compliance-ready delivery closes that gap.

3

A lean team's integration budget

A 20-40 person team scaling into new countries can't carry per-hospital integration engineering as a fixed cost. A transactional, no-code model keeps integration spend proportional to actual volume, not headcount.
