One CEO, both sides of the table

Marc Peeters ran Antwerp University Hospital (UZA) before leading Qurin — he's lived the integration and compliance headaches a hospital board weighs before adopting a new diagnostics partner.

EU compliance, US ambitions

Qurin already reaches into the US research ecosystem through ARPA-H, MIT, Boston University, and Georgia Tech. Clinical deployment means assay data needs to move compliantly on both sides of the Atlantic.

Small team, real research load

A ~16-person team is running two active R&D programs (Qatra and Qumbra) plus a federal subcontract — not a team that can build and maintain parallel EU/US data infrastructure by hand.

Who they are

Qurin is a Leiden, Netherlands-based diagnostics company developing urine-based liquid biopsy technology for non-invasive cancer detection, currently focused on primary bladder cancer in hematuria patients — spanning biomarker capture (Qatra) and clinically actionable assays (Qumbra).

Where Vorro fits

Qurin's science is the hard part, and it's done — biomarker capture and interpretive assays that work. The next hard part is operational: getting assay results into a hospital's own EHR or oncology workflow, compliantly, on both sides of the Atlantic. Vorro is the integration layer that carries that load so Qurin's team stays focused on the science.
