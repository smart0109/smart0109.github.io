Where it hurts — and what Vorro does about it
Oral oncolytics and specialty drugs stall on prior auth
Pain Prior auth and financial-assistance chasing bounces between the EHR, payer portals, and the specialty pharmacy — patients wait days to start therapy.
Vorro Vorro wires the oncology EHR to the payer/prior-auth services (CoverMyMeds, Availity) and the dispensing pharmacy so PA status, benefits, and fills sync automatically — shrinking time-to-treatment.
Radiation oncology is a silo
Pain ARIA/MOSAIQ live apart from the medical-onc EHR and billing, forcing duplicate charting and lost charges.
Vorro A bidirectional interface keeps the rad-onc record, scheduling, and charge capture in lockstep with the EHR and practice-management billing.
Outside labs and pathology arrive as faxes
Pain Results come by fax or portal and get scanned in, delaying treatment decisions and tumor-board prep.
Vorro Vorro lands HL7/FHIR lab and pathology results structured in the EHR automatically — no scanning, no re-keying.
Value-based reporting is manual
Pain OCM/EOM reporting and the tumor registry require pulling data from several systems by hand.
Vorro Vorro aggregates EHR, billing, and registry data into clean, submission-ready extracts on a schedule.
The Integration Pitch

Why Vorro, why now

New York Oncology Hematology's cancer program only works when the oncology EHR, radiation-oncology system, labs, pathology, imaging, and the payer/prior-auth side agree on the same patient. When they don't, treatment stalls and staff reconcile it by hand. Vorro is the layer that keeps every one of these systems in sync so care doesn't wait on data.

The payoff is concrete: one integration layer retires the redundant interfaces and manual data movement that quietly drain the budget, streamlining operations and taking roughly 35% out of what an organization your size spends on IT.
