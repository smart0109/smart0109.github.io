Operating Room Pilot
One-Page Overview · Perioperative Services

Less waste in the OR, fuller surgical schedules, fewer auth delays

A focused pilot that connects the systems the OR already runs so AI and automation can cut supply and tray waste, take repetitive work off a stretched team, and refill canceled cases before the block goes dark.

Prepared by Manish Agarwal for Dr. Michael Verdon, following our Sep 22, 2026 conversation. Companion to the neurosurgery coding pilot proposal.

Waste & trays

Instrument trays opened and reprocessed but barely used, and supplies pulled per case that never reach the patient. Every unused instrument still costs sterile processing labor.

Talent shortage

Schedulers, sterile processing techs and OR nurses spend hours on phone tag, manual tray counts and rework instead of patient care.

Cancellations & auths

A late cancellation or a stalled prior authorization leaves an empty OR slot, and rescheduling is manual, so the time is usually lost.

What the pilot does

One service line, one set of ORs, three workstreams. Vorro integrates the existing systems into one data layer; AWS supplies the AI agents; nothing is ripped out.

1

Tray and supply optimization

Waste

Match instrument usage per procedure and per surgeon against what is on each tray and on the preference cards. Flag instruments that are rarely used, recommend leaner trays, and track supplies opened but not used.

2

Smart rescheduling

Throughput

When a case cancels, automatically find patients who are cleared, authorized and ready, offer them the slot, and give the scheduler a ready-to-confirm list instead of a phone list.

3

Prior authorization readiness

Revenue

Check every scheduled case for auth status days ahead, surface missing documentation early, and submit electronically where the payer supports it, so no case reaches the day of surgery unauthorized.

Talking points for hospital administrators

Lead with cost and capacity, not technology. Each audience hears the number it owns.

1

CFO and finance

This pays for itself from the OR budget: tray and supply savings plus refilled cases. No new capital; the pilot runs on the AWS footprint the system already uses for Epic disaster recovery.

2

CTO and CIO

Nothing is replaced. Vorro connects Epic, tray tracking, supply chain and payers through standard interfaces, governed and monitored, so IT owns one integration layer instead of another point tool.

3

OR director and CNO

Gives time back to the team they cannot hire fast enough. Less phone tag, fewer manual counts, and no last-minute auth scrambles on the day of surgery.

4

Surgeons

Trays that match how each surgeon actually operates, and a schedule that stays full. Preference card changes are proposed from data and approved by the surgeon.

5

Low risk, fast answer

One service line, about six weeks, with a baseline and weekly numbers. The scale decision is made on the hospital's own results.

6

Builds on the coding pilot

Same data layer, same partners. OR efficiency and coding accuracy together make one cost and revenue story for the system.

Six weeks to a decision

1

Week 1: Scope and baseline

Pick the service line and ORs, pull current tray, supply, cancellation and auth data, agree targets.

2

Weeks 2 to 3: Connect and configure

Vorro connects scheduling, tray tracking, supply and payer data; AWS agents configured and reviewed with the clinical team.

3

Weeks 4 to 5: Run live

Lean-tray recommendations, auto-refill of canceled slots and auth readiness checks running, with weekly metric reports.

4

Week 6: Results and scale decision

Savings and utilization results presented to administration with the plan to expand to more ORs and service lines.
